The problem / 01

Most projects don't fail at design. They fail three months after launch.

The site looks right on the pitch deck. Then the first hundred real users arrive, an admin needs a permission nobody scoped, payments need reconciling, and the person who set up the server has stopped replying.

  • Roles get decided last. Who can approve, publish, refund or see a client record is treated as an afterthought, then retrofitted badly.
  • Nobody owns the server. The build is handed over; the deployment, certificates, mail deliverability and backups are not.
  • Payments stop at "it redirected". Webhooks, failed charges and reconciliation are where the real money problems live.
  • Compliance arrives late. Consent, data retention and NDPA obligations get bolted on after the data is already collected.
This slide earns the rest of the deck. If the prospect has been burned before, let them tell that story here — then the next slide is the answer.

Case study / 05 · Healthcare
theunforsaken.org · place.theunforsaken.org

A clinical practice, running on one platform.

A psychology practice needed more than a website: a way for clients to find the right therapist, book a real session against real availability, and for the practice to run the whole operation without a spreadsheet.

  • Three portals, one system. Separate experiences for clients, therapists and administrators, each seeing only what their role permits.
  • Scheduling that respects reality. Live availability, calendar integration, conflict prevention, and graceful behaviour when a calendar is unreachable.
  • Two service tiers served from a single platform, so the practice can reach different client segments without running two businesses.
  • Built for sensitive data under Nigeria's NDPA — consent, access control and retention designed in, not bolted on.

Process / 09

Shipped in phases, so you see it working early.

STEP 01

Scope

We map who uses the system, what each role may do, and what must be true on day one. You get a written scope with fixed deliverables before anyone writes code.

STEP 02

Build in phases

Work ships in reviewable phases on a staging link you can open any time. No six-week silences, no big reveal at the end.

STEP 03

Deploy

Production server, domain, SSL, mail deliverability and payment integration configured and tested under load before launch day.

STEP 04

Operate

Documentation, staff walkthrough, monitoring, and an agreed support arrangement. The system stays maintained, not orphaned.

Step 2 is the trust move: a staging link they can open on their own phone whenever they want.
Handover / 10

You own it. All of it.

No hostage code, no mystery hosting account, no vendor you can't leave. Everything is in your name and documented well enough that another engineer could pick it up tomorrow.

  • Source code and repository transferred to accounts you control.
  • Domains, hosting and mail registered to your organisation, with credentials handed over.
  • Deployment runbook — exactly how to update the system, written for a person, not a machine.
  • Roles and permissions matrix documenting who can do what, and where to change it.
  • Staff walkthrough and a recorded session for whoever joins later.
  • Support arrangement in writing, with defined response times.
This slide closes deals with anyone who has been held hostage by a previous developer. Ask if that's happened to them.
